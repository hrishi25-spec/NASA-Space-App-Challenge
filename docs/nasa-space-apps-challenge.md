# 2026 NASA Space Apps Challenge ## Challenge Details **Event:** 2026 NASA Space Apps Challenge **Difficulty:** Advanced | Intermediate **Subjects:** Earth Science | Software ### Challenge Description Understanding how burning activity is distributed across our planet over time, including wildfires and agricultural burns intense enough to be detected by satellite sensors, enables us to anticipate critical periods, guide monitoring efforts, and improve fire response. Satellites have tracked active fire hotspots for more than two decades, but the record is split across sensors whose data cannot be compared directly, leaving fire managers without a consistent picture of when and where burning has occurred. Your challenge is to build a web application that harmonizes these records into a **burning activity calendar** based on active fire hotspots, enabling early warning and emergency responders, scientists, and land managers to examine historical fire patterns, unusual conditions, and critical periods within a selected area of interest. **Photo credit:** The National Commission on Space Activities of Argentina (CONAE) --- ## Related Research Papers The following research papers provide relevant background, methodologies, and case studies on satellite-based active fire hotspot detection, classification, clustering, and prediction using VIIRS, MODIS, and related sensors. --- ### 1. Analysis of Forest Fire in Australia using Visible Infrared Imaging Radiometer Suite **Authors:** Sarath Raj Nadarajan Assari Syamala, Anusha Santhosh, Rameela Ramesh Department of Aerospace Engineering, Amity University Dubai, Dubai, UAE Emails: sraj@amityuniversity.ae, anushaS@amitydubai.ae, rameelaR@amitydubai.ae **Conference:** 2021 11th International Conference on Cloud Computing, Data Science & Engineering (Confluence) **DOI:** 10.1109/Confluence51648.2021.9377152 #### Abstract The recognition and acknowledgment of the wildfires are of high significance, especially in countries such as Australia due to its intense bionetwork. Prompt bushfire detection is crucial to fire management as elimination of fire is quicker and more effective when the fires are on a small scale. Immediate detection allows emergency management to compose decisions that will save lives, time, and any loss. The imperceptible escalation in satellite sensory technologies has paved the way for opportunities in forestry monitoring and management practices requiring greater fidelity data on burning and other fire activities. The use of satellite imagery for fire detection is vital for the identification of emission approximations and bio conservatives studies. This composition uses the open source Near Real Time (NRT) data provided by the active fire map of Fire Information for Resource Management System by NASA. We used the Visible Infrared Imaging Radiometer Suite (VIIRS) instrument mounted on the Suomi National Polar-orbiting Partnership (NPP) satellite that compiles and processes visible images along with infrared images that depict the geographical data. A Gridded Fire Hotspot sensor was also employed to identify the major fire hotspots of the country as well as provide data on the 2019-2020 bushfire season of Australia. In this paper, the received data is then analyzed further and monthly developments are observed and compared with the statistics. **Keywords:** VIIRS, Visible Infrared Imaging Radiometer Suite, Gridded Fire Hotspot, Fire Detection, Australia #### Key Sections Summary **I. Introduction** Fires are a natural element fueled by condensed vegetation. Wildfire spreads cause immense ecological, economic, and social expenditure. Early detection is key to suppression. Remote sensing is preferable to conventional detection due to large area coverage. Sensors used include MODIS, AVHRR, VIIRS, and Landsat. **II. Study Area** Australia (coordinates ~25.2744° S, 133.7751° E). Climate is usually hot and dry (subtropical). Landscapes range from central deserts to northeastern dense forests and southeastern mountain chains. **III. Sensors** - **VIIRS on Suomi NPP** (launched 28 October 2011): Multi-band imaging ability. Spatial resolution 750 m. Channels include I-bands (high resolution) and M-bands (moderate). Special Day/Night Band (DNB). - Technical specs: Orbit 830 km sun-synchronous; Data Rate 5.9 Mbps; Swath 3000 km. - **Gridded Fire Hotspot:** Each pixel represents a single hotspot of fire spread. Confidence intervals: low, moderate, and high. **IV. Methodology** - Elemental aspects of fire detection (heat/light emission, smoke, post-fire burns, modified vegetation). - Planck function and Wien’s displacement law for temperature detection. - Algorithm uses 375 m and 750 m channels. - Threshold tests for day/night fire pixels (e.g., BT₄ = 367 K for saturation). - Background pixel separation to avoid false alarms. - Software: NASA FIRMS (Near Real-Time data from MODIS and VIIRS). Levels 2–4 processing (swath → tiled → gridded, cloud-corrected). **V–VI. Analysis & Results (2019–2020 Black Summer Bushfires)** - Period analyzed: June 2019 – March 2020 (~240+ days). - June 2019: Hotspots concentrated in northern and south-eastern regions. - September 2019: Spread into interior and increased south-eastern hotspots. - December 2019: Vast coverage; only aerial firefighting possible. - March 2020: Most fires subsided. - Regional patterns: Northern Australia peaks in dry season (winter/spring); southern/southeastern in summer/autumn; central driven by hot dry winds. Influenced by humidity, wind, rainfall, lightning, temperature inversions. **VII. Conclusion** The Black Summer bushfires caused unprecedented damage. Satellite imagery from high-resolution VIIRS with gridded hotspots enables alerting and planning. Future work could use Semantic Reasoning, Tensor Decomposition, Cross-space information combination, data mining, process modelling, and GIS software. --- ### 2. Classification of Hotspot in East Kalimantan Forest with VIIRS Sensors using Support Vector Machine **Authors:** 1st Islamiyah, 2nd Nataniel Dengen, 3rd Eny Maria, 4th Medi Taruk Mulawarman University / State Polytechnic Agricultural, Samarinda, Indonesia **Conference:** 2018 2nd Borneo International Conference on Applied Mathematics and Engineering (BICAME) #### Abstract The specifications of the forests in Kalimantan are part of the vegetation type which is characteristic of dry lowland forests that are rich in biodiversity. The Kalimantan forest area is known as an area that has the highest vascular biodiversity in the world. Until 2014, the rate of forest loss as a source of biodiversity was always high every year. The National Polar-Orbiting Suomi Partnership (Suomi NPP) is a new generation of weather satellite imagery from NASA which currently continues to develop application algorithms for environmental monitoring. The support vector machine (SVM) method is a one-class learning machine method with artificial neural network methods that can recognize patterns from input or examples given and also include supervised learning. This study aims to analyze the effect of each parameter on the SVM method and the combination that can produce the highest accuracy, and analyze the ability of the SVM method for making hotspot maps based on the number of forest fire areas in East Kalimantan. The results of the study showed that the Support Vector Machine (SVM) method on the spectral data produced a total of 85%. **Keywords:** SVM, S-NPP Satellite Image, Hotspot, VIIRS Sensor, Classification #### Key Sections Summary **I. Introduction** Suomi NPP (2011) continues NOAA and Terra/Aqua MODIS missions. VIIRS has similar spectral bands to MODIS but better spatial resolution (22 channels: 5 I-bands, 16 M-bands, Day/Night channel). Active Fire algorithm builds on MODIS MOD14 (Collection 6), improved to 375 m / 350 m resolution by Schroeder et al. (2014). **II. Literature Review** - **SVM:** Supervised learning based on Structural Risk Minimization (SRM). Finds optimal hyperplane separating classes. Margin maximization; support vectors are closest points. Linear and non-linear (kernel) variants. - Formula: \( g(x) := \operatorname{sgn}(f(x)) \) where \( x, w \in b \). **III. Methodology** - Data: VNF, SPOT-5, MODIS; field survey and Ministry of Environment & Forestry blackout data. - Buffering (single + dissolve) at fire points. - Validation against burnt area (SPOT), fire smoke (MODIS), field points. - Indices: NDVI (Huete method), NBR (Key & Benson). - Threshold determination: \( \mu + 1\sigma \), \( \mu \), \( \mu - 1\sigma \). - Identification of burned areas via NDVI/NBR/reflectance change models. **IV. Results** - 156 reference burned-area polygons from SPOT-5. - Reflectance, NDVI, NBR changes pre- vs post-fire quantified. - NBR with \( \mu + 1\sigma \) achieved highest accuracy (~63.5% in one reported model set; overall SVM spectral accuracy reported as 85%). - Conclusion: SVM (linear kernel, selected parameters) suitable for land-use/hotspot classification at 1:100,000 scale (accuracy >85% in some experiments). --- ### 3. The Development of Satellite-based Active Fire Database and Hotspot Clustering to Enhance Forest Fire Management Practices in Thailand **Authors:** Woranut Chansury, Nuntikron Kitratporn, Anusorn Rungsipanich, Teansiri Moolchan (GISTDA) Chatchaya Buaniam, Itthirit Pintong, Natthanon Chaiyasak (Department of National Parks, Wildlife and Plant Conservation) Bangkok, Thailand **Conference:** 2024 Geoinformatics for Spatial-Infrastructure Development in Earth and Allied Sciences (GIS-IDEAS) **DOI:** 10.1109/GIS-IDEAS63212.2024.10991155 #### Abstract This research aims to develop centralized database of satellite-based active fire hotspot and develop hotspot clustering algorithm to support firefighting operation. The first result is the active fire hotspot database which is automatically retrieving data from 4 satellites under Moderate Resolution Imaging Spectroradiometer: MODIS (Aqua and Terra) and Visible Infrared Imaging Radiometer Suite: VIIRS (Suomi NPP and NOAA20). The second result is the hotspot clustering model based-on DBSCAN technique which showed 99% agreement with the location of actual fire event. The percentage of overlapping area was 76.72% in forest. The clustering of hotspots will assist in identifying extensive fire zones for forest rangers, facilitating their entry to these regions to extinguish the fire and avert its propagation. This strategy will alleviate the effects on forest regions, fauna, and ecosystems. Furthermore, at a national level, it can facilitate policy-making by assisting agencies in prioritizing and distributing resources for efficient fire management. **Keywords:** Active fire hotspot, Fire clustering, DBSCAN, Forest fire control #### Key Sections Summary **I. Introduction** Wildfires in Thailand mainly Dec–Apr (peak March), northern region most affected. Human activities primary trigger. Firefighting operations rose significantly 2017–2020. Shift from MODIS (1 km) to VIIRS (375 m) increased hotspot count, overwhelming operations. Need for clustering of spatially close hotspots into meaningful fire events. **II. Materials and Methods** - **Data:** FIRMS (MODIS Aqua/Terra + VIIRS Suomi-NPP / NOAA-20). - **Centralized Database:** Automated retrieval, quality filtering, local timezone conversion, UTM Zone 47 (WGS84), land-use overlay, data dictionary. - **DBSCAN Clustering:** - Hyperparameters: \( \varepsilon \) (search radius), minPts. - Optimal: \( 530.3 < \varepsilon \leq 750 \) m (based on 375 m grid diagonal); selected \( \varepsilon = 550 \) m, minPts = 3, temporal window 12 h. - Core / border / noise points. - **Delineation:** Convex hull + 187.5 m buffer. - **Validation:** Overlap with Sentinel-2 burn scars (NBR / dNBR). 54 samples; 99% of clusters intersected burn scars; forest overlap average 76.72% (median 83.50%). **III. Results** - Database supports 4 satellites (Table of launch years, frequency, source). - 5-year (2017–2021) Suomi-NPP VIIRS: 799,809 hotspots → 49,479 clusters (mean ~4.14 hotspots/cluster; max 109). - Positively skewed distribution (nearly 50% of clusters have only 3–4 points). - Spatial hotspots highest in northern provinces (Mae Hong Son, Chiang Mai, etc.); lowest/none in some southern provinces. **IV. Conclusions** Centralized multi-sensor database + DBSCAN clustering (550 m, minPts=3, 12 h) produces actionable fire zones for operational use and policy. Best suited to forest (not agricultural) fires. --- ### 4. A Comparative Analysis of Deep Learning Approaches for Fire Hotspots Prediction in Thailand **Authors:** Sansern Phawandee, Nuanprang Sangurai*, Narongsak Putpuek Rajabhat Rajanagarindra University, Chachoengsao, Thailand Nagul Cooharojananone Chulalongkorn University, Bangkok, Thailand **Conference:** The 23rd International Symposium on Communications and Information Technologies (ISCIT2024) #### Abstract Northern Thailand faces a recurring ecological threat: forest fires. These fires, prevalent during the dry season, spread rapidly and consume vital forest fuels. Human activities exacerbate the problem, with agricultural burning of forests, grasses, and crop residues contributing to the fire regime. These fires not only alter forest structure, but also produce harmful PM10 and PM2.5 particles that threaten public health. The purpose of this work is to perform a comparative analysis of two deep learning approaches, including single and hybrid, for fire hotspots prediction. The dataset was acquired from NASA’s MODIS and VIIRS S-NPP satellite, covering the period from January 2012 to February 2024. The dataset values were pre-processed by coding and normalization. The pairwise correlation coefficient between each feature and the number of hotspots was calculated and then sorted in descending order. Based on these correlation values, a dimensionality reduction step was performed to reduce the number of features from 4 to 2 for each experiment. The prepared dataset was then divided into 80% and 20%. Four deep learning models were trained and three error metrics were used to objectively evaluate the model performance. The investigation showed that the single approach using LSTM algorithm had promising results in identifying fire hotspots in Thailand, especially when using data from the VIIRS S-NPP dataset with two or three attributes: confidence, bright_ti5, and frp or confident and bright_ti5. **Index Terms:** Deep Learning, Fire Hotspots, Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM), Gate Recurrent Unit (GRU), Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) #### Key Sections Summary **I. Introduction** Northern Thailand dry-season fires (late Jan–Apr). Agricultural burning contributes. Monitoring relies on NASA FIRMS (MODIS + VIIRS S-NPP). Time-series attributes: Latitude, Longitude, Brightness, Acq_Date/Time, Confidence, Bright_T31/T5, FRP, etc. Goal: compare single (LSTM, GRU) vs hybrid (CNN-LSTM, CNN-GRU) deep learning for hotspot prediction. **II. Related Work** ARIMA, C5.0 decision trees, Random Forest / SVC / Gradient Boosting, LSTM (>95% accuracy in Indonesian studies), FU-NetCastV2 CNN for spread prediction, hybrid CNN-LSTM. **III. Background** - **LSTM:** Gating (forget, input, output) overcomes vanishing/exploding gradients of vanilla RNNs; retains long-term dependencies. - **GRU:** Simplified LSTM (update + reset gates); fewer parameters. - **CNN:** Feature extraction via sliding windows on 1-D time series; hybridized with LSTM/GRU. **IV. Experimental Method** - **Datasets:** - MODIS: 321,483 records (2012–2024). Attributes include Brightness, Confidence, Bright_t31, FRP, etc. - VIIRS S-NPP: 2,065,030 records. Attributes include Bright_t4, Bright_t5, Confidence, FRP, etc. - **Preprocessing:** Daily aggregation of hotspot counts; coding + normalization; correlation-based feature ranking; dimensionality reduction (4 → 2 features); 80/20 train/test split. - Models trained; evaluated with three error metrics. - Best performance: Single LSTM on VIIRS S-NPP using combinations of confidence, bright_t5, and FRP. --- ## Summary for Challenge Participants These papers collectively demonstrate: | Theme | Key Techniques / Sensors | Relevance to Challenge | |-----------------------------|-------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------| | Multi-sensor harmonization | MODIS + VIIRS (FIRMS NRT) | Core need for a consistent burning calendar | | Hotspot detection | VIIRS 375 m / 750 m channels, thresholds | Active fire detection foundations | | Clustering of events | DBSCAN (ε ≈ 550 m, minPts=3, 12 h window) | Grouping discrete hotspots into fire events | | Classification / Mapping | SVM, NBR, NDVI, reflectance change | Burned-area validation & land-cover context | | Time-series prediction | LSTM / GRU / CNN hybrids | Forecasting critical periods & early warning | | Operational databases | Automated multi-satellite pipelines | Backend for a web application | **Suggested starting points for the web application:** 1.
